Community building burden: You have to BUILD the community before revenue flows - 6-12 months of negative cash flow while recruiting members
Utilization problem: Equipment sits idle most of the time, but you're paying for it 24/7
Mission drift: Constantly wrestling with "should we serve hobbyists or focus on workforce development?" to satisfy different funders

Fatal Flaw: You're selling access to a place before the place exists. Classic chicken-egg - no space without members, no members without space.

Core Economics:

Costs: Vehicle, generator, CNC/laser equipment, materials inventory, fuel, event fees, permits

Startup capital: $30K-50K (vehicle, equipment, inventory, permits, insurance)
Event dependency: You only make money when physically present at events
Throughput bottleneck: CNC takes 10-20 minutes per item - can only serve 20-40 customers per 8-hour event
Weather/seasonal: California outdoor events are seasonal, plus weather kills weekend plans
Commoditization pressure: You're competing with other event vendors on impulse purchases - hard to command premium pricing
Setup/teardown overhead: 2-3 hours of logistics for every 6-8 hours of selling time

Fatal Flaw: Your unit economics require high volume, but your production speed creates low throughput. You're trapped between "craft maker" pricing (takes too long) and "mass production" volume (can't do it fast enough).

Error correction is the other piece. QR codes have four levels - L (Low, 7% recovery), M (Medium, 15%), Q (Quartile, 25%), and H (High, 30%). Higher error correction means the code can be partially damaged or obscured and still scan, but it also requires more modules for the same data. You're probably using M or Q level, which is the sweet spot for reliability without bloating the code size.
Modern phone cameras have gotten dramatically better at close-focus and image processing, which is why codes that would've been unscannable five years ago work fine now. The software does edge detection and perspective correction automatically, so even slightly worn or angled codes usually scan.
